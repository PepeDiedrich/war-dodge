# Level 3 action guide — Reconsider travel

A Level 3 Travel Advisory means there are serious safety or security risks.
It is not automatically an evacuation order and the appropriate response
depends on the country-specific advisory, your exact location, and local
official instructions.

## Do this promptly

1. Read the complete Travel Advisory and its risk indicators, not only the
   level number. Check official local emergency messages and the nearest
   embassy or consulate.
2. Tell a trusted person where you are, how to contact you, and your intended
   route if you leave. Agree on a check-in schedule.
3. Keep passport, visas, IDs, tickets, essential medicines, phone charger,
   power bank, keys, cash in local currency, and a small amount of backup food
   and water together in a ready-to-carry bag.
4. Save offline maps and the contact details for local emergency services,
   your embassy or consulate, accommodation, transport providers, and trusted
   contacts. Keep devices charged.
5. Check practical departure options now: airline or rail status, border
   requirements, road conditions, fuel, and at least two safe routes. Do not
   rely on a government evacuation being available.
6. Review medical and evacuation insurance, including exclusions and the
   insurer's emergency number.

## Decide whether to leave

Leave early when it is safe and practical if the full advisory, local official
instructions, credible security reporting, or your personal circumstances make
remaining unsafe. Do not travel into an active danger area simply to depart,
and do not use unsafe roads or border crossings. Follow instructions from local
authorities and transport operators.

For immediate danger, contact local emergency services first. U.S. citizens
can enroll in the Smart Traveler Enrollment Program (STEP) and contact the
nearest U.S. embassy or consulate. Other nationals should use their own
foreign ministry or consular service.

## Do not wait for Level 4

Level 4 is “Do not travel.” It can coincide with very limited official ability
to help. If you choose to depart, earlier and orderly travel may be safer than
waiting for transport, roads, supplies, or communication to deteriorate.

Sources: [State Department crisis response guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/help-abroad/crisis-response.html) and [Travel Advisory levels](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories.html).
