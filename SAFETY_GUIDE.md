## Level 3: Imminent Threat / Pre-War Window (Reconsider Travel)

This is the operational departure window. In military context, Level 3 often signals that foreign embassies are beginning voluntary departures or non-combatant evacuation preparations. Commercial flights and open borders still function, but logistics will begin shutting down.

**Decide: Exit vs. Shelter:**

**If leaving:** Leave now using commercial air or passenger rail while borders are normal and ticketing systems still work. Do not wait for Level 4.

**If staying:** Immediately move away from high-value targets (military bases, munitions depots, government ministries, major communication towers, central energy plants, and primary dual-use airports).

**Convert Funds to Physical Cash:** Banking grids, SWIFT/SEPA gateways, and domestic ATMs are frequent early casualties of cyberattacks and domestic bank runs. Withdraw physical local and hard currency.

**Comms Redundancy:** Set up battery-powered or hand-crank AM/FM shortwave radios to receive emergency broadcast bands if local cell towers are jammed or powered off.

**Top Off Stockpiles:** Expand home provisions to at least 30–60 days of food and water purification capability.

## Level 4: War / Critical Hazard (Do Not Travel / Ordered Departure)

Kinetic conflict has begun, strikes are imminent, or foreign embassy personnel have been ordered to evacuate. Airspace is typically closed to civilian traffic, and borders may be heavily restricted or militarized.

**Shelter in Place:** If you missed the departure window, traveling on roads during the initial phase of warfare carries high risk due to missile strikes, unexploded ordnance, ambushes, and checkpoints. Stay indoors, preferably below ground level (basement, interior rooms away from exterior glass).

**Blackout Protocol:** Keep light discipline at night. Black out windows to prevent visible signatures from the street or air.

**Civilian Demarcation:** If moving on foot or in vehicles during pauses:

Avoid any military-style clothing, tactical colors (camo, olive drab, coyote tan), or bags. Dress in standard civilian attire.

Clearly mark vehicles with civilian signs (e.g., white cloth or markers) only if local security conditions warrant it.

**Follow Humanitarian Corridors:** Rely exclusively on officially announced, monitored humanitarian transit corridors rather than ad-hoc escape routes across contested frontlines.⏎
